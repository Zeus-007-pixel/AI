15.1The idea: draw the whole trajectory, then fix it

The capstone simulated forward in time and then adjusted α. Dymos turns this around. It uses a method called collocation:

  1. Split the flight time into segments and place a few points, called nodes, in each.
  2. Let the optimizer guess the state and the control at every node, all at once, as if drawing the whole trajectory freehand.
  3. At every node, Dymos runs your ODE component (vectorized over all nodes, Day 12) to get the rates the physics demands. It compares them with the slope of the drawn curve. The mismatch is called a defect.
  4. The defects become equality constraints (they must be 0). The optimizer adjusts the drawing until the physics is satisfied everywhere and the objective is as good as possible.
h │ o──o──o o = node: the optimizer's guess of the state there │ \ │ o──o at every node: slope of the drawn curve │ \ vs. rate computed by your ODE │ o──o difference = "defect" → must become 0 └──────────────────────── t

There's no simulation inside the optimization loop, the derivatives are exact and sparse (Days 11 and 12), and the control can take a different value at every node. That's what makes it fast and reliable for problems like your HGV.

15.2The complete program

New file d15_glider.py. It's the longest file of the course, but you know every Python and OpenMDAO piece in it. Type it carefully:

{{code:d15_glider.py}} {{out:d15_glider.py|grep=Optimization terminated|Iterations|Optimization Complete|Simulating|Done simulating|flight time|alpha between}}

(The real output is longer. Dymos and OpenMDAO also print coloring reports and one harmless warning about the constraint on q at the first node, which can't change because the starting state is fixed.)

Block 1: the ODE (lines 8–52)

Exactly the GliderODE you ran by itself on Day 12. Dymos creates it with the right num_nodes and connects states, controls and parameters to the inputs with the same names.

Block 2: problem and optimizer (lines 56–58)

p.driver = om.ScipyOptimizeDriver(optimizer='SLSQP', maxiter=300)
The same driver as Day 10, allowed up to 300 iterations.
p.driver.declare_coloring()
Day 12's sparsity idea, applied to the whole problem. This problem has hundreds of design variables and constraints, and coloring makes their derivatives cheap.

Block 3: trajectory, phase and transcription (lines 61–63)

traj = p.model.add_subsystem('traj', dm.Trajectory())
A Trajectory is a Group that holds phases. Add it to the model like any subsystem.
dm.Radau(num_segments=15, order=3)
The transcription: how time is chopped up. 15 segments of order 3 (a few nodes each). More segments means more accuracy but a bigger problem. Radau is a good default.
traj.add_phase('glide', dm.Phase(ode_class=GliderODE, transcription=…))
A phase is one stretch of flight with one ODE. Note ode_class=GliderODE with no brackets: you give Dymos the class (the blueprint, Day 5), and Dymos creates the objects itself. Multi-stage flights, such as boost then glide, use several phases.

Block 4: time (line 66)

phase.set_time_options(fix_initial=True, duration_bounds=(10, 5000), duration_ref=100, units='s')
The start time is fixed. The duration is free, so the optimizer decides how long the flight lasts, between 10 and 5000 s. duration_ref is scaling, as on Day 10.

Block 5: states (lines 69–73)

phase.add_state('h', rate_source='h_dot', units='m', fix_initial=True, lower=0, ref=1e3)
The state's name must match the ODE input's name ('h'). rate_source names the ODE output holding its rate. fix_initial=True pins the starting value to your initial guess (Block 9). lower=0 keeps the glider above ground at every node. ref is scaling.
'x' isn't an ODE input
That's fine. Nothing in the physics depends on downrange distance. Dymos still integrates it from x_dot.

Block 6: the control (line 76)

phase.add_control('alpha', units='deg', lower=-5, upper=15, rate_continuity=True)
α becomes a history: a value at every node, chosen by the optimizer. You may work in degrees; Dymos converts to radians for the ODE (Day 8 units). rate_continuity=True keeps the history smooth between segments.

Block 7: parameters (lines 79–80)

phase.add_parameter('m', units='kg', val=500.0, opt=False)
Fixed numbers sent to the ODE at every node. opt=True would let the optimizer choose it, turning this into vehicle design plus trajectory optimization at once.

Block 8: constraints, objective and extra outputs (lines 83–91)

phase.add_boundary_constraint('h', loc='final', equals=0.0, units='m')
A boundary constraint applies at one end of the phase ('initial' or 'final'). Here: end on the ground.
phase.add_path_constraint('q', upper=20000.0, units='Pa', ref=1e4)
A path constraint applies at every node. For your HGV, this is where heat-rate, dynamic-pressure and g-load limits go. It can constrain any ODE output, which is why the ODE outputs q.
phase.add_path_constraint('alpha', upper=15.0, units='deg')
See "Good to know" in 15.4.
phase.add_objective('x', loc='final', ref=-1e4)
Maximize final downrange (negative ref, Day 10).
phase.add_timeseries_output('q')
Also record q in the results.

Block 9: initial guess (lines 96–101), after p.setup()

phase.set_time_val(initial=0.0, duration=600.0)
Start at t = 0 and guess a 600 s flight. The optimizer will change the duration.
phase.set_state_val('h', [5000.0, 0.0])
A guess as a straight line from the first value to the second, spread over all nodes. Because of fix_initial=True, the first value is the real starting condition: released at 5000 m.
phase.set_control_val('alpha', [3.0, 3.0], units='deg')
Guess a constant 3° to start. A sensible guess matters a lot: starting from zeros (speed 0!) would make the physics divide by zero.

Block 10: run (line 104)

dm.run_problem(p, simulate=True, make_plots=True)
Runs the optimization (like run_driver). simulate=True then re-flies the result with an ordinary step-by-step integrator (like Day 7) using the optimal control, to confirm the answer obeys the physics between nodes too. make_plots=True saves plots of every variable to d15_glider_out/reports/traj_results_report.html.

Blocks 11–12: results and plots (lines 107–130)

p.get_val('traj.glide.timeseries.x', units='km')
The timeseries holds every state, control and extra output at every node, by full path: trajectory name, phase name, timeseries, variable.
t[-1, 0]
Timeseries values are 2D arrays with one row per node and one column (Day 6). [-1, 0] is the last row, which is the end of the flight.
The two plt.figure() blocks
Day 7 plotting. plt.figure() starts a new, separate figure.

15.3Reading the answer like an engineer

  • Range: 60.35 km, against 58.89 km for the best constant α on Day 14. Letting α vary over time buys about 1.5 km.
  • For most of the flight α sits at about 6.4°, the best-L/D angle you found on Days 6 and 10. Dymos rediscovered it on its own.
  • At the start, α is low: the glider trades its extra speed for distance before settling into the efficient glide.
  • At the end, α rises sharply: a final flare that turns the remaining speed into a little extra distance just before touching down.

Physics you can explain is the best evidence an optimization is right. Always look for it.

15.4Good to know before you go further

Control bounds at segment ends

With the Radau transcription, the control's lower/upper bounds are enforced where the control is a design variable. The value at the very end of each segment is extrapolated and can slip past the bound. While writing this course, a heavier glider showed α = 19.2° at the final node despite upper=15. The fix is a path constraint on the control, which checks every node: phase.add_path_constraint('alpha', upper=15.0, units='deg').

  • Check simulate=True. In the HTML report the simulated curves should lie on top of the optimized points. If they drift apart, use more segments.
  • Scaling and guesses matter even more than on Day 10. Give every state a ref, and guess a trajectory that's at least roughly physical.
  • Bigger problems (your 6-state HGV) are much happier with the IPOPT optimizer than SLSQP. It comes with pyoptsparse, which is easiest to install with conda (conda install -c conda-forge pyoptsparse). Then use p.driver = om.pyOptSparseDriver(optimizer='IPOPT').

15.5From this glider to your HGV

  1. Swap the ODE. Use your HGV_ODE, with 6 states (r, θ, φ, v, γ, ψ) and 2 controls (α, σ). Add one add_state per state and one add_control per control. Replace declare_partials('*', '*', method='cs') with declare_coloring(wrt='*', method='cs') (Day 12).
  2. Real atmosphere. Dymos ships a US 1976 standard atmosphere component: from dymos.models.atmosphere import USatm1976Comp. Put it in a Group with your ODE (Day 9), or use np.interp on a table (Day 6).
  3. Real aerodynamics. Compute L and D from ρ, v, S and CL/CD tables in α and Mach, so α finally matters.
  4. Limits as path constraints: heat rate (the Sutton–Graves formula from Day 5), dynamic pressure and load factor (Day 12), all as ODE outputs.
  5. The footprint. Solve the same problem several times with different objectives: maximize final θ (downrange), minimize it, and maximize ±φ (crossrange). Each solution becomes the initial guess for the next.
  6. Study the official example. Dymos includes a Space Shuttle reentry problem with the same kind of physics, in dymos/examples/shuttle_reentry in your installed package, and in the online Dymos documentation.
